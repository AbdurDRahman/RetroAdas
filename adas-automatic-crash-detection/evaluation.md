### Converted to false alarms per hour

The paper reports 735,000 harsh-driving events from 123,000 vehicles over 48 h → 5.98 harsh events per vehicle. Our detector only ever fires on a harsh event (nothing below the 1.8 g pre-trigger is scored), so false alarms/hour = FPR × harsh events/hour:

| basis | harsh events / h | false alarms / h | hours per false alarm |
|---|---|---|---|
| per vehicle wall-clock hour (48 h window) | 0.124 | 0.0031 | 322 |
| per driving hour, assuming 8 h driving/day | 0.373 | 0.0093 | 107 |

Caveat: the commercial fleet in the paper drives more aggressively than a private car, and the harsh-event trigger threshold is not published; both push the real-world rate *down*, so these are conservative.

## 4. Compute cost

`engine_update` on this laptop (gcc -O2, x86-64): **52 ns/sample** over 2,000,000 synthetic samples, including the Python→C batch call. At 100 Hz the budget is 10,000,000 ns/sample; even if the ESP32 (240 MHz Xtensa LX6, single-precision FPU) were 300× slower it would use 0.2% of it.
